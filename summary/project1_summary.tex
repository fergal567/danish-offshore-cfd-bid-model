{\bfseries\Large Reverse-engineering Vattenfall's 2026 Danish offshore wind bids}\par
\vspace{4pt}
{\itshape What do DKK 504/MWh (Nordsøen Midt) and DKK 542/MWh (Hesselø) imply about cost, return and wind?}\par
\vspace{4pt}
{\color{grey} Fergal Israel \quad·\quad Independent analysis from public data \quad·\quad October 2026, revised November 2026}\par

\vspace{8pt}
\rule{\linewidth}{0.8pt}\par\vspace{6pt}
Vattenfall's prices are 3--6\,\% below my base-case break-even, a gap within the model's own uncertainty. They are consistent with a real after-tax return of about 4.5--5\,\%, inside Vattenfall's own range for wind, and need the DEA's FID-2030 costs (20\,MW-class turbines). With the DEA's own capture ratio, break-even rises to 591--606.\par

\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\textwidth}
\head{The bigger risk: the payment cap}
The cap funds an average top-up of DKK 167/MWh at Nordsøen Midt but 313 at Hesselø. In my low-price stress case it runs out at Nordsøen Midt around 2047 (roughly DKK 5.5bn unpaid). Hesselø never uses more than 76\,\%.
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\textwidth}
\head{What it means for the next tender}
Nordsøen Syd (bids due October 2028) has a cap of DKK 6.6bn, about DKK 75/MWh of top-up. Its bidders will have to price the risk of low power prices explicitly. \note{(My interpretation.)}
\end{minipage}
